# Priority 2 diagnosis: healthy-surge false alarms and container recreation (read-only, on existing data)

Written 2026-10-18, before any change. Evidence sources: the 225 benchmark rows, the exported truth records (`results/benchmark-v1/`), the 72 training runs, the frozen `models-v1` artifacts, and `docs/postmortems/surge-diagnosis-data.md` (generated by `scripts/diag_surge.py`, offline, IN-SAMPLE for the models). Nothing was retrained, tuned or run against the stack for this report.
What is **not** available: the benchmark's own feature windows, Prometheus series and incident confidences (the rows keep none; the stack's Prometheus retention is 2 h), so every statement about *why* the live brain fired is a hypothesis unless marked as observed.

## What was observed (facts)

1. **Autopilot, `traffic_surge`, 5 runs:** the first incident opened 12.9-15.3 s after the injection (the surge lasts 40 s; the run window is 60 s). In 4 of 5 runs it was diagnosed `memory_leak` and the autopilot recreated a container (`inventory` three times, `web` once) at about 31 s, inside the surge; verification then reported "recovered". In the fifth the incident was abstained. Source: rows plus truth files.
2. **The same symptom appears in another brain.** `prototype_rules` (ported rules, no learned models) also diagnosed `memory_leak` in 4 of its 5 surge runs (seeds 107, 117, 127, 147 escalated as `memory_leak`; seed 137 stayed `open/pending`), without acting. `threshold_restart` opened nothing during the surge window (only a `pending` incident about 69 s after injection, after the scoring window, which is the evaluator's end-of-run restore). So the trigger is common to two different diagnosers, which points to the **input signal** (memory-related features and the healthy limits around them) more than to the learned classifier alone.
3. **Second incidents in surge runs** open 63-66 s after injection, i.e. after the evaluator's `t_end` (60 s); they are the evaluator's restore (web restarted), outside scoring. They are not the false alarm.
4. **Training data has one surge level.** All 8 training surge runs and all 5 benchmark surge runs used `extra_rps_per_thread = 25` (4 threads, about +75 rps to `web` and `inventory`: about 6 to 80 rps). The evaluator draws 25 or 40 from the seed (`rng.choice([25, 40])`); for the 13 seeds used (1007, 1107, ..., 1707 and 107, 117, ..., 147) the draw was 25 every time; seeds such as 3001, 3003, 3004 would give 40 (computed by simulating the draw; the live evaluator was not asked). All training surges target `web`; every surge lasts 40 s. So the models never saw a second intensity, a different duration or a surge aimed at another service.
5. **The frozen models are quiet on the training surges (in-sample):** 0 of 144 windows per service flagged by the detector, every window classified `healthy`, no learned limit exceeded (section 2 of the data file). So the false alarms are not explained by the surges the models were trained on; they happen on a surge of the same statistical level in a different run.
6. **The learned healthy limits on memory are very tight.** `inventory` `mem_share` hi limit is 0.2603 (about 69.9 MB of 256 MiB), against a healthy median of about 65.3 MB and training-surge windows peaking at 0.2517 (about 67.5 MB): about 2.4 MB of headroom. `web` 75.0 MB limit against a 66.8 MB median. `mem_slope_share_per_min` hi for `inventory` is 0.062 against a surge p95 of 0.030 (section 5 of the data file).
7. **Healthy memory does not drift across the training runs** (linear fit about -0.01 MB per 100 seeds for `inventory`, 0.02 for `web`; section 6), so uptime drift is not visible in training.

## Hypotheses, with evidence for and against

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Answers to the specific questions

- **Did the training data have enough traffic variation?** No. One surge level (25), one target (`web`), 8 runs, 40 s each; the healthy classes total 16 of 72 runs. The models have no evidence about higher or lower surges, shorter or longer ones, or surges on other services.
- **Are the features normalised for traffic?** Partly: the four `cpu_per_req_*` features are divided by request rate. Latency, error ratio and memory features are ratios or z-scores against the service's own healthy baseline and are not rate-normalised; `rps_ratio` and `rps_z` are inputs.
- **Root cause proven?** **No.** H1 is the leading hypothesis and has the strongest supporting evidence (two brains, thin limits), but it is not confirmed because no live memory or feature data exist for the benchmark runs.

## Consequence, and a design problem to state plainly

This points to a weakness in how the detector's healthy limits are learned: for a feature whose healthy range is almost constant (memory), the margin is proportional to that tiny range, which yields a limit only a couple of MB above healthy. That is a design problem in the limit-setting, not a surge-specific bug; a fresh-data retrain with a minimum-width rule or a wider margin would address it (rules for that go into the v2 pre-registration and are fitted only on fresh runs). It does not contradict the stated priorities, but it means fixing "surges" may change how limits are learned for every service, which is a larger change than a surge patch.

## Next, in order (no change to the system until pre-registered)

1. Run the read-only capture (H1-H3, H6) on fresh non-benchmark seeds at both surge levels with the brain only logging (watch-only), recording memory against the learned limit. Script-only measurement.
2. Add the onset-window replay (H5) to `scripts/diag_surge.py`.
3. Pre-register v2 (Amendment 4) with this diagnosis as its stated motivation; any retraining uses fresh runs only (rules G3b).

## Addendum: read-only capture, 6 of 12 runs (surge level 25 only; stopped by low memory, not restarted)

Source: `results/surge-capture/raw/` (6 runs, seeds 3600, 3601, 3602, 3603, 3605, 3606), rendered in `docs/results/surge_capture.md`. The brain was the v2 build in watch-only mode, so it logged what it saw and never acted. These are non-benchmark seeds.

**Observed (facts):**
- An incident opened in **6 of 6** surge runs. In every one the v2 diagnosis record lists `limits_exceeded` containing **`mem_share`** (one also `mem_slope_share_per_min`, one also `cpu_per_req_ratio`). That is direct evidence that the memory limit is what trips the detector (hypothesis H1), not something inferred from another brain.
- During the surge `web` memory rose from about 67 MB to about 77 MB (0.285-0.289 of its limit, against a learned limit of 0.2794), and `inventory` rose from 66-73 MB to about 76 MB (0.283-0.286, against 0.2603). In the training surges the same services rose by about 1 MB (`web`) and 2.4 MB (`inventory`) and stayed under their limits. So the live surge raises memory by several times what the training surges did.
- At rest, **before** the surge, `orders` already sat at 73.6 MB (0.2742 of its limit) against a learned limit of 0.2738 (about 73.5 MB), and against a training median of about 69.9 MB. `orders` exceeded its learned limit in all 6 runs without any surge on it, and `inventory` was at 66.5-72.6 MB before the surge. Healthy memory in the live stack is higher than it was in the training runs, by a few MB per service, which is as large as the headroom the limits leave.
- With the answered label `healthy` plus a memory-limit exceedance, the engine abstains rather than guesses (5 of 6 incidents ended `insufficient_evidence`; 1 diagnosed `memory_leak` and stayed open). In v1 the same situation was diagnosed `memory_leak` and acted on in 4 of 5 autopilot runs.

**What this changes:**
- **H1 is now supported by direct evidence** (limits exceeded in 6 of 6 incidents); H6 (the stack's healthy memory differs from training) is supported for `orders` and `inventory`; H3/H2 (intensity and normalisation) are not separable yet because the level-40 runs were not made.
- The cause of the higher live memory (state growth in the persistent SQLite volumes after many hours of load, container age, or something else) is **not established** here; it was not measured.
- The detector's healthy limits are tied to absolute memory levels learned over one short collection window. Any drift of a few MB, or a transient load-driven rise of several MB, moves a healthy service outside them. That is the design weakness stated earlier, and the capture shows it is not rare: it happens in every surge run and at rest for `orders`.
